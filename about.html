I've been on both ends of it.

I've been the person held together by duct tape, in organisations that couldn't see the invisible load they were placing on people.

I've also built and rebuilt systems across two decades in IT and security, and before that through a scatterplot of unrelated jobs. Enough to understand how environments shape minds, and not the other way around.

Kind Mechanics is where that gets distilled: structural explanations, practical methods, design principles, and field notes from turning chaotic systems into coherent ones.

I'm Brian McCallion. I work in security engineering by day and run Kind Mechanics alongside it, from Limerick, Ireland.

Principles

Clarity as the Standard

Mechanism over vibes. Say what is actually happening, in language anyone can use.

Kindness as the System

Built into how things work, so it holds up on hard days. People are already trying hard.

Usefulness as the Goal

Everything here should be applicable in real life, on an ordinary working day.

Human-readable by default

If a person needs training to parse it, it is not finished yet.
