Research

Kind Mechanics carries the workplace and cognition strand of my research: the part concerned with how environments and neurodivergent minds interact.

Executive paralysis and initiation failure

Why you can know what to do, tell yourself to do it, and still not move. This work draws on cognitive control theory and transdiagnostic clinical models, but the aim stays practical: find the state variables that shut initiation down, then turn that into brief checks and interventions that reduce shame and restore movement.

Demand avoidance as institutional misfit

Treating ADHD and demand avoidance as a design problem in workplaces and support systems rather than individual pathology: the gap between the motivation a system assumes and the motivation a person actually has. Preprint on SocArXiv.

Burnout and shutdown as predictable outcomes

Neurodivergent burnout and shutdown treated as the expected result of environments that ignore cognitive architecture and cumulative load, rather than as personal failure arriving from nowhere.
